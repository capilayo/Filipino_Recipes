Kinilaw na Tuna

Fresh tuna "cooked" in calamansi and coconut vinegar with ginger, red onion, and chili — Filipino ceviche at its finest.

?? Seafood? 15 min prep?? No cook?? 4 servings? Easy

?? Description

Kinilaw na Tuna is the Filipino version of ceviche — fresh, sashimi-grade tuna "cooked" in calamansi juice and spiced with ginger, onions, chili, and coconut vinegar. A refreshing, no-cook dish popular in Mindanao and Visayas.

?? Ingredients

  • 500g fresh sashimi-grade tuna, cut into 1-inch cubes
  • ½ cup coconut vinegar (sukang tuba) or white cane vinegar
  • ¼ cup calamansi juice (about 10–12 calamansi)
  • 1 thumb-size ginger, peeled and julienned
  • 1 medium red onion, thinly sliced
  • 2–3 pcs siling labuyo (bird's eye chili), sliced
  • 1 tsp salt · ½ tsp black pepper · 1 tbsp fish sauce (patis), optional
  • 1 stalk spring onion, sliced (for garnish) · 1 small cucumber (optional)

????? Instructions

  1. Rinse tuna cubes quickly in a small amount of vinegar. Drain and pat dry.
  2. In a bowl, combine coconut vinegar and calamansi juice. Add tuna cubes and toss gently. Let sit for 5–10 minutes — the acid will begin to "cook" the fish.
  3. Drain most of the liquid (keep about 2–3 tbsp). Add ginger, red onion, chili, salt, pepper, and patis.
  4. Toss gently. Taste and adjust seasoning.
  5. Serve immediately, garnished with spring onion.

?? Tips & Tricks

  • Use only the freshest sashimi-grade tuna — freshness is everything in kinilaw.
  • Don't marinate too long or the tuna will become too firm (over-cooked by the acid).
  • Sukang tuba (coconut vinegar) gives the most authentic Visayan flavor.
