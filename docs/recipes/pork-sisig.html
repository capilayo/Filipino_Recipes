Pork Sisig

Sizzling chopped grilled pork belly with calamansi, chili, and egg on a hot cast-iron plate — king of pulutan.

🍖 Main Dish⏱ 20 min prep🕐 40 min cook👥 4 servings⭐ Medium

📝 Description

Pork Sisig is one of the Philippines' most iconic dishes — a sizzling, savory-sour medley of chopped grilled pork belly, seasoned with calamansi, chili, and onions, served on a scorching hot cast-iron plate. Born in Pampanga, sisig has evolved from bar chow staple to a national treasure.

🛒 Ingredients

  • 500g pork belly, boiled and grilled (or use pork ears/face if available)
  • 200g pork liver, boiled (optional but traditional)
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 3–4 pcs siling labuyo (bird's eye chili), sliced
  • 3 tbsp calamansi juice · 2 tbsp soy sauce · 1 tbsp oyster sauce
  • 1 tbsp mayonnaise (optional, for creamier version)
  • 2 tbsp cooking oil or butter · Salt and pepper to taste
  • 2 eggs (for topping, optional) · 1 green onion, sliced (for garnish)

👨‍🍳 Instructions

  1. Boil pork belly until tender (about 30 minutes). Drain and grill/broil until lightly charred. Let cool.
  2. Finely chop the grilled pork (and liver if using) into small pieces.
  3. Heat a cast-iron skillet until very hot. Add butter or oil. Spread chopped pork — let it sizzle without stirring for 2–3 minutes to get crispy edges.
  4. Add onion, chili, calamansi juice, soy sauce, and oyster sauce. Toss and cook another 2–3 minutes. Add mayonnaise if using.
  5. Crack 1–2 eggs on top, let cook slightly then mix in before serving. Garnish with green onion.
  6. Serve immediately on the hot sizzling plate with steamed rice or as pulutan.

💡 Tips & Tricks

  • The key to great sisig is the char — grill the pork until properly blackened for smoky flavor.
  • Cast-iron sizzling plate is a must for the authentic restaurant-style experience.
  • Add a tiny amount of butter to the hot plate for extra richness.
