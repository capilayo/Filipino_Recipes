Dinuguan

Savory Filipino pork blood stew — tangy, dark, and deeply flavored. Best with puto!

?? Main Dish? 15 min prep?? 45 min cook?? 4–6 servings? Medium

?? Description

Dinuguan — affectionately called "Chocolate Meat" by curious foreigners — is a rich, savory Filipino stew of pork cooked in pig's blood seasoned with vinegar, garlic, and chili. One spoonful of this dark, tangy, deeply savory stew is enough to make anyone a believer. Best paired with puto!

?? Ingredients

  • 500g pork kasim (shoulder) or pork belly, cubed
  • 200g pork intestine or liver, cleaned and sliced (optional)
  • 1½ cups fresh pig's blood — stirred to prevent clotting
  • ? cup white vinegar · 1 cup water or pork broth
  • 5 cloves garlic, minced · 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 2 pcs long green chili (siling haba) · 1 tsp whole black peppercorns
  • 2 tbsp cooking oil · Fish sauce or salt to taste · 1 tsp sugar (optional)

????? Instructions

  1. Heat oil. Sauté garlic and onion until fragrant.
  2. Add pork and cook until browned on all sides.
  3. Add intestines or liver (if using) and cook for 3–5 minutes.
  4. Pour in vinegar — do not stir for 2–3 minutes to let acidity cook off.
  5. Add water/broth, peppercorns, and chili. Simmer covered for 20 minutes.
  6. Slowly pour in pig's blood while stirring constantly to prevent lumps.
  7. Continue stirring as the blood cooks and thickens the sauce (about 10–12 minutes).
  8. Season with fish sauce and sugar. The sauce should be thick and dark. Serve with puto or rice.

?? Tips & Tricks

  • Stir the blood continuously as you add it — clumps form fast if you stop.
  • Adding vinegar early and not stirring removes the harsh "raw vinegar" taste.
  • Dinuguan actually improves overnight — cook it the day before a big event!
