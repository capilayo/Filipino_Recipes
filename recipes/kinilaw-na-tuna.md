# Kinilaw na Tuna (Filipino Tuna Ceviche)

## Description
Kinilaw na Tuna is the Filipino version of ceviche — fresh, sashimi-grade tuna "cooked" in calamansi juice and spiced with ginger, onions, chili, and coconut vinegar. It's a refreshing, no-cook dish popular in Mindanao and Visayas, served as both an appetizer (pulutan) and a main dish. Simple, bold, and incredibly fresh.

---

## Ingredients
*(Serves 4)*

- 500g fresh sashimi-grade tuna, cut into 1-inch cubes
- ½ cup coconut vinegar (sukang tuba) or white cane vinegar
- ¼ cup calamansi juice (about 10–12 calamansi)
- 1 thumb-size ginger, peeled and julienned
- 1 medium red onion, thinly sliced
- 2–3 pcs green siling labuyo (bird's eye chili), sliced (adjust to taste)
- 1 tsp salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 tbsp fish sauce (patis), optional
- 1 stalk spring onion, sliced (for garnish)
- 1 small cucumber, thinly sliced (optional)

---

## Instructions

1. **Wash the tuna** — Rinse tuna cubes quickly in a small amount of vinegar to remove any fishy odor. Drain and pat dry.
2. **Marinate** — In a bowl, combine coconut vinegar and calamansi juice. Add tuna cubes and toss gently. Let sit for 5–10 minutes — the acid will begin to "cook" the fish, turning it opaque.
3. **Season** — Drain most of the liquid (keep about 2–3 tbsp). Add ginger, red onion, chili, salt, pepper, and patis if using.
4. **Toss gently** — Mix until well combined. Taste and adjust seasoning.
5. **Serve immediately** — Garnish with spring onion and serve with steamed rice or as pulutan with cold beer.

---

## Tips
- Use only the freshest sashimi-grade tuna — freshness is everything in kinilaw.
- Don't marinate too long or the tuna will become too firm (over-cooked by the acid).
- Sukang tuba (coconut vinegar) gives the most authentic Visayan flavor.

5. Ihain agad — fresh pa fresh!
