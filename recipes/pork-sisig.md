# Pork Sisig (Sizzling Pork Sisig)

## Description
Pork Sisig is one of the Philippines' most iconic dishes — a sizzling, savory-sour medley of chopped grilled pork face (maskara), ears, and belly, seasoned with calamansi, chili, and onions, served on a scorching hot cast-iron plate. Born in Pampanga, sisig has evolved from bar chow staple to a national treasure. With a cracked egg on top and a cold beer beside it, life is complete.

---

## Ingredients
*(Serves 4)*

- 500g pork belly, boiled and grilled (or use pork ears/face if available)
- 200g pork liver, boiled (optional but traditional)
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 3–4 pcs siling labuyo (bird's eye chili), sliced
- 3 tbsp calamansi juice (about 6–8 calamansi)
- 2 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1 tbsp mayonnaise (optional, for creamier version)
- 2 tbsp cooking oil or butter
- Salt and pepper to taste
- 2 eggs (for topping, optional)
- 1 green onion, sliced (for garnish)

---

## Instructions

1. **Prep pork** — Boil pork belly in water with salt and pepper until tender (about 30 minutes). Drain and grill or broil until lightly charred on all sides. Let cool.
2. **Chop** — Finely chop the grilled pork (and liver if using) into small pieces. This is the heart of sisig — the finer the chop, the better.
3. **Sizzle** — Heat a cast-iron skillet or heavy pan until very hot. Add butter or oil. Spread chopped pork in a single layer and let it sizzle without stirring for 2–3 minutes to get crispy edges.
4. **Season** — Add onion, chili, calamansi juice, soy sauce, and oyster sauce. Toss and cook another 2–3 minutes. Add mayonnaise if using and mix well.
5. **Egg on top** — Crack 1–2 eggs on top, let cook slightly then mix in before serving. Garnish with green onion.
6. **Serve** — Serve immediately on the hot sizzling plate with steamed rice or as pulutan.

---

## Tips
- The key to great sisig is the char — grill the pork until properly blackened for smoky flavor.
- Cast-iron sizzling plate is a must for the authentic restaurant-style experience.
- Add a tiny amount of margarine/butter to the hot plate before adding sisig for extra richness.
