# 🍙 Biko

**Category:** Dessert / Kakanin | **Prep Time:** 10 mins | **Cook Time:** 50 mins | **Servings:** 12–15 pieces | **Difficulty:** Easy

---

## 📝 Description

Biko is a classic Filipino sticky rice cake (kakanin) made with glutinous rice cooked in coconut milk and brown sugar, topped with golden caramel latik or coconut curd. It's a staple at every Filipino celebration, town fiesta, and meryenda table — sweet, chewy, and utterly irresistible. Pag may Biko, masaya ang bawat handaan!

---

## 🛒 Ingredients

**Biko:**
- 2 cups glutinous rice (malagkit), washed and soaked for 1 hour
- 2 cups coconut milk
- 1 1/2 cups water
- 1 1/2 cups dark brown sugar (or muscovado)
- 1/2 tsp salt

**Latik (Caramel Coconut Topping):**
- 1 cup coconut cream (kakang gata)
- 1/2 cup dark brown sugar

---

## 👨‍🍳 Instructions

**Cook the rice:**
1. In a pot, combine glutinous rice, 1 cup coconut milk, and water. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly until the rice is half-cooked and liquid is mostly absorbed (~15 minutes).

**Make the sauce:**
2. In a separate pan, combine remaining 1 cup coconut milk with brown sugar and salt. Bring to a simmer, stirring until sugar dissolves.
3. Add the half-cooked rice to the coconut-sugar sauce. Stir continuously over low heat until the mixture is very thick and rice is fully cooked (~15–20 minutes).

**Make the latik topping:**
4. In a small pan, combine coconut cream and brown sugar. Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly until it caramelizes into a thick, golden-brown sticky syrup (about 15 minutes).

**Assemble:**
5. Transfer biko into a greased baking pan or banana-leaf-lined bilao. Flatten evenly.
6. Pour latik topping over the biko and spread evenly.
7. Let cool completely before slicing and serving.

---

## 💡 Tips & Tricks

- **Soak the glutinous rice** beforehand for faster, more even cooking.
- Use **muscovado sugar** for a deeper, more complex caramel flavor.
- Biko keeps well at room temperature for 1–2 days — cover with plastic wrap.
