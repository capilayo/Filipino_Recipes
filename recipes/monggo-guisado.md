# Monggo Guisado (Sautéed Mung Bean Soup)

## Description
Monggo Guisado is a Filipino staple — a hearty mung bean stew sautéed with garlic, onion, tomatoes, and pork (or shrimp), enriched with malunggay or ampalaya leaves. This budget-friendly comfort dish is traditionally served on Fridays and is a beloved feature of the Filipino weekly menu. Thick, filling, and packed with nutrition.

---

## Ingredients
*(Serves 6)*

- 2 cups dried mung beans (monggo), soaked overnight or 4 hours
- 200g pork belly, cut into small cubes (or substitute with shrimp/smoked fish)
- 1 cup malunggay leaves (or ampalaya/bitter melon leaves)
- 1 medium onion, chopped
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
- 2 tbsp fish sauce (patis)
- 1 tbsp cooking oil
- 4–5 cups water or pork broth
- Salt and pepper to taste
- 1 tbsp shrimp paste (bagoong alamang), optional

---

## Instructions

1. **Cook monggo** — Drain soaked mung beans. Place in a pot with fresh water (enough to cover by 2 inches). Boil for 30–40 minutes until soft and beginning to break apart. Set aside.
2. **Sauté aromatics** — In a large pan, heat oil over medium heat. Sauté garlic until golden. Add onion and tomatoes; cook until softened, about 3 minutes.
3. **Add pork** — Add pork cubes and cook until lightly browned, about 5 minutes. Season with patis.
4. **Combine** — Pour in the cooked monggo along with its liquid. Add broth if needed for consistency. Simmer for 10 minutes.
5. **Finish** — Add malunggay leaves and stir. Simmer 2 more minutes. Adjust seasoning with salt and pepper. Serve hot with steamed rice.

---

## Tips
- Soaking the mung beans overnight cuts cooking time significantly.
- Smoked fish (tinapa) makes a delicious swap for pork — very classic combination.
- Add a dollop of bagoong on top for extra umami punch.
