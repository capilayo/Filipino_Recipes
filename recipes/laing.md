# 🌿 Laing (Bicol Laing)

**Category:** Vegetable | **Prep Time:** 10 mins | **Cook Time:** 45 mins | **Servings:** 4–5 | **Difficulty:** Easy

---

## 📝 Description

Laing is the beloved Bicolano dish of dried taro leaves (gabi) slow-cooked in rich coconut milk with chili, shrimp paste, and pork or shrimp. It's creamy, smoky, spicy, and intensely savory — a dish from the Bicol region that has won hearts all over the Philippines. Once you taste authentic Laing, nothing else compares.

---

## 🛒 Ingredients

- 200g dried taro leaves (dahon ng gabi), crushed — **do not wash**
- 2 cans (800ml total) coconut milk (gata)
- 1/2 cup coconut cream (kakang gata)
- 200g pork belly or shrimp (peeled)
- 3–5 pcs red chili (siling labuyo), whole or sliced
- 2 pcs long green chili (siling haba)
- 2 tbsp bagoong alamang (shrimp paste)
- 5 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 medium onion, sliced
- 1 thumb ginger, julienned
- Salt to taste

---

## 👨‍🍳 Instructions

1. In a wide pot, combine coconut milk, garlic, onion, ginger, and pork. Bring to a simmer.
2. **Do not stir** — let the coconut milk cook and reduce for about 15 minutes.
3. Add the crushed dried taro leaves on top. **Do not stir yet.**
4. Let the taro leaves cook in the steam for 15–20 minutes until wilted and softened.
5. Now stir gently, mixing the taro leaves into the sauce.
6. Add bagoong alamang, chili, and coconut cream. Stir gently.
7. Simmer on low heat for another 10–15 minutes until the sauce is thick and creamy.
8. Season with salt and serve hot with white rice.

---

## 💡 Tips & Tricks

- **Never wash the dried taro leaves** — it removes the natural starch that thickens the dish.
- The secret is **patience** — don't rush the cooking; low and slow builds the flavor.
- For extra heat, add more **siling labuyo** — Bicolanos love it fiery!
